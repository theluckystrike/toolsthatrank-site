Your CTR by position, measured against our own fleet

A page's click through rate is its clicks divided by its impressions, and both numbers come straight from Search Console's Pages report. Below you can enter your own row and see where it sits on a curve we measured ourselves.

Across our own 52 properties over the last 90 days, pages at positions 1 to 10 earned 0.5559% per impression on average, while pages at positions 51 to 100 earned 0.078%. Read on 2026-10-10 from the Search Console searchAnalytics/query endpoint, window 2026-07-10 to 2026-10-07. These readings come from one operator's properties and are not an industry benchmark. If you want the metric's definition, Google's help answer on position and impressions defines both terms.

The concentration check

The largest property, belikenative.com, carries 64.14% of pooled impressions, so every band is also reported with it removed. Pooled, then without that property: 1 to 10 0.5559% and 0.8587%; 11 to 20 0.3297% and 0.7942%; 21 to 30 0.344% and 0.3825%; 31 to 50 0.1671% and 0.21%; 51 to 100 0.078% and 0.0817%. Both readings ship side by side and we do not explain why they differ.

The property extremes

Scoped to properties with at least 20 clicks in the window (13 of 52): the lowest CTR among them is 0.0814% at an average position of 21.01; the highest is 25% at an average position of 9.7; across those 13 properties the Spearman rank correlation between average position and CTR is 0.165. Property level averages mix page mixes and query types, so they say nothing about any single page.

How we measured this

We measured our own Search Console properties through the Google Search Analytics API via the searchAnalytics/query method, based on a 90 day window from 2026-07-10 to 2026-10-07, with Search Console's finalisation lag leaving the last three days unread, read on 2026-10-10 across 52 verified properties. We ran two routes, dimensions [page] and dimensions [page, date]; the daily route rebuilds each page's position as an impression weighted mean, and both routes place pages into the same bands and agree on every band to the printed precision. An independent Python recomputation over the same checksummed export reproduced the totals and the per property table. Both routes come from the same provider, so their agreement cannot catch an upstream Google error. CTR was kept to four decimals in the arithmetic and printed as the token value; percentages appear to two decimals on the page. No per property file is published yet, and query text is never collected or published. Counts and averages are recomputed in code, never guessed, and every figure here was fetched, recomputed, and checked before a human approved the page.

For background on the metric itself, Google's help answer defines position and impressions, and Google's Search Central post on performance data filtering and limits notes that anonymized queries are omitted from query tables and that the interface export stops at 1,000 rows of data (read 2026-09-18). Public explainers cover the metric too, for example SEOTesting's average position page (read 2026-09-18), which says Google averages the position across all impressions. The new part of this page is the interactive placement of your row against this measured fleet, not the curve itself.

What this cannot tell you

A different window gives different numbers. The curve is built from property level aggregates, so a page whose own mix of queries differs from its property's mix can sit anywhere on the curve. CTR says nothing about why a click happened or whether it was useful. A band is an average over many pages, not a target.

No ranking, traffic, or revenue outcome is promised. The operator sells the software described here and owns every site in the dataset. These figures come from one operator's properties, not from a representative sample of the web. Search Console is authenticated, so readers cannot recheck the private source readings without access. Figures are read on the stamped date and change as Search Console reprocesses data.
