Compare one page with its band

Open the Pages tab of the Search Console Performance report and pick one page. Copy its average position, impressions and clicks. An example row is loaded. Replace it with your own.

Enter an average position, impressions and clicks, then press Calculate.

The numbers you type stay in your browser. Nothing is fitted between bands. A decimal position is rounded first, the same rule the dataset uses, so 20.5 reads as 21. A position outside 1 to 100 returns no reading.

The measurement

Every band, with the counts behind it

A band measures pages by their average topmost position across all their queries. It is therefore not the same measurement as a published CTR at rank curve, and the two should not be compared.

One property, belikenative.com, supplies 64.14% of the fleet's impressions (921,701 of 1,436,949). So each band is shown twice, once for all 52 properties and once with that property removed. Read them together.

Where the curve does something unexpected

Pooled, the 21 to 30 band (0.3440%) sits above the 11 to 20 band (0.3297%). With the largest property removed the order is the usual one, 0.7942% then 0.3825%. Every band also reads higher with that property removed. We report the shape and do not explain it, because this export measured no cause.

Why these rates differ from our position bands page

Our position bands page reads 34 properties over 2026-05-15 to 2026-08-12. This page reads 52 properties over 2026-07-10 to 2026-10-07. The two windows overlap from 2026-07-10 to 2026-08-12. The rate in every band differs between the two pages. They are two separate readings of a small fleet, and neither corrects the other.

Method

How we measured this

We measured these bands on 2026-10-10 against the Google Search Console Search Analytics API, method searchAnalytics/query, with read only access, on the 52 verified properties in the operator's account. The window is 90 days, 2026-07-10 to 2026-10-07, and ends three days before the read date. All 52 properties returned data.

Route one

Query grouped by page. Search Console returns one average position per page for the window. We round it to a whole number, put the page in the band that holds it, then sum clicks and impressions inside the band.

Route two

Query grouped by page and date. Each page's position is rebuilt as the impression weighted mean of its daily rows, then banded the same way. The export records the same impressions, clicks and page count as route one in all five bands.

The rate

Pooled. The band's clicks divided by the band's impressions, not a mean of page rates. A page with many impressions moves the rate more than a page with few.

Filters

No minimum impression filter was applied. 4,385 of the 4,386 page rows fall in a band. The export marks no property as cut short by the row limit.

Band sums and property totals do not match

The five bands sum to 1,479,871 impressions and 6,306 clicks. The same window read at property level gives 1,436,949 impressions and 6,228 clicks, and the sum of the daily rows gives the same two figures. The band sums are 2.99% and 1.25% higher. Search Console counts impressions by page differently from impressions by property, and its report overview says totals can differ between the two. We print both and adjust neither. The 64.14% share uses the property totals. The band rates use the page rows.

What the checks cannot catch

Both routes call the same API, so their agreement cannot catch an error on Google's side. A separate script recomputed every figure on this page from the saved export before release. That checks our arithmetic. It does not check the export step. Query text is never collected or published, and no per property file is published.

The calculator divides a band's clicks by its impressions without rounding. It rounds only what it displays, to four decimals.

Limits

What this cannot tell you

It cannot tell you what CTR your page should have. A band rate is what our pages recorded, pooled, in one window. A different window gives different numbers, as the two band pages on this site show.

The sample is one operator's fleet, and most of it is small. 12 of the 52 properties recorded no click in the window, and 13 recorded 20 or more. Among those 13, property CTR ran from 0.0814% (40 clicks on 49,160 impressions, average position 21.01) to 25.0000% (37 clicks on 148 impressions, average position 9.7). A band rate hides a spread that wide.

A page's average position mixes every query the page appears for, so two pages in one band can face different queries. CTR also says nothing about why a click happened or whether it was useful. A band is an average over many pages, not a target.
