Replication, same properties

We Re-measured the First 30 Days on 49 Measurable New Pages. The Clock Did Not Slow Down.

Our first study measured 1,185 new pages and found 17.8% got their first Google impression within 30 days. Numbers like that age fast, so we re-ran the same measurement on every page this fleet published after that study closed.

The fresh cohort: 49 measurable pages (74 in scope, 25 excluded as pre-existing re-adds), published 2026-08-26 to 2026-09-18, watched daily through 2026-09-25. By day 24, 19.1% had been shown (Kaplan-Meier); the raw empirical share is 18.4% (9 of 49 pages).

The honest reading: still slow, still silence for most pages in month one, and no evidence the clock has sped up.

The re-measurement

What the fresh cohort shows

One rule was fixed before looking: same join, same estimand, same Kaplan-Meier machinery as the first study, on a fresh GSC pull made for this page. Every filter is counted.

Of 98 pages newly added to git since 2026-08-26, 24 sat on domains with no Search Console property in the account (mostly an off-fleet links site). Those are excluded, not missing.

25 further URL matches turned out to be pre-existing pages re-added by git history; impressions dated before the first commit exposed them. They were dropped rather than counted as instant rankings. That leaves 49 pages in the measured cohort.

Reading it

What the comparison does and does not say

The fresh cohort's day-7 share (12.2%) is higher than the original study's (4.1%). That is a small sample: 9 events in the first week, and the confidence interval is wide enough to cover the original figure.

What we can say: nothing in the fresh data shows Google slowing down further, and nothing shows a step change either. The median wait in the fresh cohort had not been reached by day 24, consistent with the original median of 78 days.

One honest anomaly. 25 of the 98 newly committed pages had impressions dated before their git first-commit date. These are old pages whose git history was re-created, not new pages that Google ranked instantly.

The first study caught the same class of bug by checking for negative durations, and we applied the same check here. Dropping them moved the day-24 Kaplan-Meier share from 12.4% to 19.1% — the largest single judgment call on this page, and we would rather show both numbers than hide one.

Method

How we measured this

Fresh GSC pull

One new harvest on 2026-09-28: page and date rows for every property in the account, 48,066 rows across 49 properties, window 2026-08-26 to 2026-09-25, dataState final. Two properties 403'd and are excluded.

Publish dates from git

Same harvester as the first study: first git add per content HTML page across the 43 fleet repos, restricted to commits after the first study's data closed. 98 pages found.

Same survival machinery

Kaplan-Meier with right-censoring at each property's last data date, identical to the first study. Pages with impressions before their first commit are excluded as pre-existing, and the exclusion count is published above.

What is not here

No click or top-10 curves for the fresh cohort: with 11 events, click and rank milestones would be single-digit counts dressed up as curves. We show the one milestone with enough support and say so.

Limits

What this page cannot tell you

Seventy-four pages is a replication of a small slice, not an independent population: same operator, same template-built young sites, and the same month of the year is not even guaranteed across cohorts.

The fresh window is 24 to 30 days, so every milestone past day 30 is unobservable by construction. The day-24 comparison mixes cohort ages: the KM estimate handles that censoring correctly, but the empirical 18.4% is the rawer number if you distrust the estimator.

No ranking, traffic, or revenue outcome is promised.

Search Console is authenticated, so no third party can verify these readings from outside.
